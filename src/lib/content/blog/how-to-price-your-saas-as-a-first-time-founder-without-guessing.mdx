Pricing feels like guesswork.

Most founders either:

- Copy competitors.
- Undercut aggressively.
- Pick a random number.
- Avoid raising prices out of fear.

Pricing is not creativity.

Pricing is positioning.

# The Core Pricing Principle

Price based on value created.

Not effort spent.

Not feature count.

Not competitor averages.

Ask:

What measurable improvement does my product create?

If your SaaS:

- Saves 5 hours per month,
- Prevents billing mistakes,
- Reduces operational errors,
- Generates revenue directly,

Then price relative to impact.

---

# The Value Anchor Method

Define:

1. Financial value created.
2. Time saved.
3. Risk reduced.
4. Revenue unlocked.

If your product saves:

- $200/month in labor,
Pricing at $29/month is conservative.

If it prevents a $500 mistake,
Pricing at $49/month is rational.

Value anchors pricing.
Not insecurity.

---

# The Three-Tier Structure

Early SaaS should avoid complex pricing.

Use:

Basic  
Pro  
Advanced  

Each tier must:

- Reflect increased value.
- Unlock clear additional benefit.
- Avoid confusing limits.

Do not:

- Gate critical functionality artificially.
- Add random feature fragmentation.
- Create 6+ pricing plans.

Clarity improves conversion.

---

# Willingness-to-Pay Testing

Instead of guessing:

- Present price confidently.
- Observe behavior.
- Measure churn.
- Measure upgrade rate.
- Measure cancellation reason.

Your users will tell you.

If nobody buys:
You priced too high or positioned poorly.

If everyone buys instantly:
You likely priced too low.

Iterate calmly.

---

# Avoid Undervaluation

First-time founders often:

- Fear rejection.
- Fear being “too expensive.”
- Fear losing early users.

Undervaluation creates:

- Low-quality customers.
- Higher churn.
- Lower perceived value.
- Harder future increases.

It is easier to lower than to raise.

Start rational.
Not timid.

---

# Pricing and Retention

Retention depends on:

- Perceived fairness.
- Predictability.
- Clear value mapping.

If users feel surprised by billing,
churn increases.

If subscription lifecycle is unstable,
trust declines.

See:
Stripe Subscription Mistakes That Kill SaaS Startups.

---

# When to Raise Prices

Raise prices when:

- Retention is strong.
- Users express high dependency.
- Support requests are value-based.
- Competitors price significantly higher.

Do not raise prices randomly.

Signal changes clearly.
Grandfather early users if necessary.

---

# Common Pricing Mistakes

- Overcomplicated tiers.
- Unlimited free plans.
- Feature gating that confuses users.
- Emotional discounts.
- Lifetime deals that destroy long-term revenue.

Pricing should support stability.

Not short-term excitement.

---

# The Simplicity Rule

If you cannot explain your pricing in one sentence,
it is too complex.

For example:

“$29/month for independent founders who want a stable production-ready SaaS foundation.”

Clarity converts.
