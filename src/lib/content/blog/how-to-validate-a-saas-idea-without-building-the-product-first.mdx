Interest

- Friends saying “cool idea”
- Likes on social media

Validation means:

Clear problem recognition  
Clear willingness to pay

# The Evergreen Validation System

We use five structured layers.

Do not skip steps.

---

## Layer 1 — Problem Clarity

Before testing demand, define:

- Exact target niche
- Specific workflow pain
- Current workaround
- Cost of inefficiency

If you cannot describe the pain in one sentence, you are not ready.

Example structure:

“Freelance designers waste X hours per week doing Y because Z.”

Clarity precedes validation.

---

## Layer 2 — Problem Interviews

Do not pitch.

Ask:

- How do you currently solve this?
- How often does this happen?
- What is frustrating about the current solution?
- Have you tried to fix it before?
- What did you try?

Look for:

- Emotional intensity
- Repeated patterns
- Explicit dissatisfaction
- Time or money cost

Avoid:

- Leading questions
- Hypothetical pricing questions
- Overexplaining your idea

You are testing pain, not features.

---

## Layer 3 — Messaging Validation

Create a simple page that explains:

- The problem
- The promised outcome
- The specific transformation
- The type of person it’s for

No feature list.

No architecture talk.

Clear outcome only.

AI can help you draft positioning copy, but do not let it invent fake confidence.

Test messaging clarity first.

If people do not understand the value, the idea is not ready.

---

## Layer 4 — Landing Page Signal Testing

Your validation page should include:

- Clear headline
- Pain articulation
- Outcome description
- Early access or waitlist CTA
- Optional pricing anchor

Track:

- Conversion rate
- Scroll depth
- Message replies
- Direct responses

Signal thresholds vary by niche, but weak signals look like:

- Generic signups without engagement
- No replies to outreach
- No follow-up questions
- No willingness to test

Strong signals look like:

- Direct replies
- Clarifying questions
- Early access requests
- Requests for launch timeline

Interest without follow-up is noise.

---

## Layer 5 — Pricing Validation

This is where most founders hesitate.

You must test price early.

Options:

- Display price range on landing page
- Offer founding discount
- Ask direct “would you pay X?” after interview
- Collect refundable deposits

If no one reacts to price, demand is weak.

If people react strongly but continue conversation, signal strengthens.

You are not optimizing price.

You are testing payment intent.

---

# Decision Thresholds

Before building, confirm at least:

- 5–10 strong problem confirmations
- Multiple unsolicited follow-up questions
- Clear pricing tolerance range
- Early access interest
- Specific use-case clarity

If you do not meet these thresholds, refine.

Do not build prematurely.

---

# The Role of AI in Validation

AI helps with:

- Drafting landing copy
- Generating interview questions
- Structuring outreach
- Refining messaging clarity

AI does not:

- Confirm demand
- Replace interviews
- Create willingness to pay

Validation is behavioral, not textual.

---

# Common Validation Mistakes

1. Building before interviews.
2. Testing features instead of outcomes.
3. Over-interpreting weak signals.
4. Avoiding price discussion.
5. Validating with friends instead of target users.

Validation must feel slightly uncomfortable.

If it feels safe, it is probably superficial.

---

# When to Move From Validation to Build

Only after:

- Problem clarity
- Confirmed demand
- Defined single revenue feature
- Defined initial pricing model

Then you move to structured build.

If ready, use a disciplined execution model:

/kits/saaskit

Build after validation.
Not during.

---

# Final Thought

Validation reduces risk.

It does not eliminate it.

But skipping validation multiplies risk.

The goal is not to prove your idea right.

The goal is to expose it to reality early.

Build after behavior confirms value.

Not before.
