Most SaaS products don’t fail because of bad code.

They fail because no one wanted them.

Before you open your editor.
Before you install a boilerplate.
Before you ask AI to generate anything.

You must validate demand.

This is the structural framework for doing that.

This is about discipline, not optimism.

---

# 1. Start With a Single Pain, Not a Platform

Do not validate:

> “An AI-powered productivity suite.”

Validate:

> “Automated Stripe reconciliation report for small agencies.”

Specific beats ambitious.

Validation requires precision.

---

# 2. Reduce to One Revenue-Capable Feature

Ask:

- What is the smallest feature someone would pay for?
- Can this exist without dashboards, complexity, or scale?
- Does it solve a clear measurable pain?

If your idea requires:

- 5 features to feel complete
- Multiple integrations
- Complex UI flows

It is not yet minimal enough.

---

# 3. Identify a Specific Audience

Not:

- “Founders”
- “Developers”
- “Creators”

Instead:

- Portuguese accountants under 10 employees
- Indie hackers selling digital templates
- Shopify stores with 100+ SKUs

The narrower the audience, the clearer the validation signal.

---

# 4. Demand Testing Without Code

You do not need a product.

You need:

- A landing page
- A clear value proposition
- A pricing anchor
- A call-to-action

Test:

- Email signups
- Demo requests
- Pre-order deposits
- Direct outreach conversations

Do not test:

- Likes
- Compliments
- “That sounds cool”

Signal is commitment.

Noise is politeness.

---

# 5. Pre-Sell When Possible

Pre-selling forces truth.

Options:

- Early-access pricing
- Founding member discount
- Limited beta seats
- Refundable deposit

If no one is willing to commit financially:

You do not have validation.

You have interest.

They are not the same.

---

# 6. Analyze Signal vs Noise

Strong signals:

- Direct payment
- Repeat follow-ups
- Specific feature requests
- Clear problem articulation

Weak signals:

- “Let me know when it’s ready.”
- Social media praise
- Non-committal encouragement

Validation requires measurable commitment.

---

# 7. Risk Assessment Matrix

Before building, score:

- Problem severity (1–10)
- Audience clarity (1–10)
- Willingness to pay (1–10)
- Competitive saturation (1–10)
- Distribution access (1–10)

If total score is weak, pause.

Do not build emotionally.

---

# 8. Define Kill Criteria Early

Before starting, define:

- If fewer than 10 serious leads → pivot
- If zero pre-sales after 30 days → kill
- If cost of acquisition too high → reconsider niche

Without kill criteria, founders drift into sunk-cost fallacy.

Structure protects you from yourself.

---

# 9. When to Move From Validation to Building

You move forward when:

- Problem is clearly articulated
- People commit attention or money
- Audience is reachable
- Feature scope is constrained
- Risk profile is acceptable

Then you build.

Not before.

---

# Final Thought

Non-technical founders often over-focus on:

“How do I build this?”

The better question is:

“Should I build this?”

Validation is not glamorous.

But it is profitable.

Discipline before development.

Always.
