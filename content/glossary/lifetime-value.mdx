<p>If you are building SaaS as a non-technical founder, terms like <strong>Lifetime Value</strong> can feel like insider language. That creates hesitation at exactly the moment you need clear decisions. This glossary page is designed to remove that friction. You do not need to become a developer to use this term correctly. You only need a practical mental model, a few warning signs, and one clear next action.</p>

<p>Think of SaaS building like running a service business that happens to be software-powered. The labels matter because they shape pricing, priorities, and weekly execution. When you understand <strong>Lifetime Value</strong> in simple language, you reduce guessing and make stronger founder decisions with less stress.</p>

<h2 id="definition">What is Lifetime Value in SaaS?</h2>

<p><strong>Definition:</strong> Lifetime Value, often called LTV, estimates how much recurring gross revenue one customer generates before they churn.</p>

<p>Lifetime Value matters because it helps founders decide how much they can safely spend on acquisition and support. You can treat this term as a decision tool, not a buzzword. If your weekly choices improve this area, your business usually becomes easier to operate and easier to grow.</p>

<h2 id="simple-explanation">Simple explanation for non-technical founders</h2>

<p>If CAC is what you pay to start the relationship, LTV is what that relationship returns over time.</p>

<p>A useful founder question is: "How does Lifetime Value change what I should do this week?" Keep the answer practical. Focus on one clear workflow, one customer segment, and one measurable outcome. Overcomplicated frameworks are less useful than a simple operating rule you can repeat every week.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Use <strong>Lifetime Value</strong> to clarify what to prioritize now, not someday.</li>
  <li>Translate the term into customer language your team can act on.</li>
  <li>Review the term in your weekly planning so decisions stay consistent.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="real-world-example">Real-world example</h2>

<p>If a customer usually stays 20 months on a $50 monthly subscription, the gross lifetime revenue is roughly $1,000 before costs and refunds.</p>

<p>The key lesson is that clarity beats complexity. In most early-stage companies, the founder does not need more tooling first. The founder needs a repeatable rule for how decisions are made. When <strong>Lifetime Value</strong> is understood by everyone involved, handoffs become cleaner and execution gets faster.</p>

<h2 id="mistakes">Common mistakes founders make with Lifetime Value</h2>

<h3>1) Using optimistic retention assumptions</h3>
<p>Inflated LTV guesses lead to overconfident spending. Use observed retention data whenever possible.</p>

<h3>2) Ignoring margin differences</h3>
<p>Revenue is not profit. Consider support and delivery cost when using LTV for decisions.</p>

<h3>3) Treating LTV as static</h3>
<p>LTV changes as pricing, onboarding, and product value evolve. Refresh the estimate regularly.</p>
