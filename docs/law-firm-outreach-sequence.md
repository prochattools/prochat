## Purpose

Use the MikeOSS legal demo and Loom as a conversation starter with local law firms.

The goal is not to sell the entire ProChat OS platform immediately.

The goal is to discover:

```text
Which intake, document, or admin workflow law firms actually hate doing manually.
```

## Positioning boundary

Do not make the ProChat website law-firm-specific.

Law firms are the first direct outreach wedge only.

```text
Public brand = ProChat OS, business-agnostic Agentic Workflow OS
Law-firm outreach = specific validation wedge using MikeOSS demo
```

## Core law-firm message

```text
ProChat OS helps document-heavy firms turn messy client information into structured admin outputs, with human approval first.
```

More concrete:

```text
Messy client emails, PDFs, notes, forms, and attachments go in. Structured intake summaries, missing-information checklists, task lists, and draft follow-ups come out.
```

Safety line:

```text
This does not provide legal advice. It helps with document/admin workflow preparation for human review.
```

## Target profile

Start local and relationship-driven.

Best first targets:

- small to mid-sized local law firms
- firms with visible document-heavy practice areas
- firms that likely handle many client inquiries or intake documents
- firms where admin speed matters
- firms where the owner/partner is reachable
- firms that look modern enough to consider software but not so large that procurement blocks a conversation

Possible practice areas to test:

- employment law
- immigration law
- family law
- contract/business law
- personal injury
- real estate/legal conveyancing, if document-heavy locally
- small business advisory law

Avoid first:

- very large firms with procurement barriers
- firms with no reachable decision maker
- firms that only want legal-specific platform guarantees
- any workflow requiring immediate real client data before trust is built

## Research notes to capture

When reviewing a firm website, look for clues such as:

- free consultation forms
- document upload portals
- intake forms
- many practice pages
- high-volume client types
- FAQ-heavy pages
- language around responsiveness
- language around document review
- team size and admin/support staff
- contact form complexity

Do not over-research. The goal is enough context for a relevant first message.

## Discovery call structure

Target length: 15–25 minutes.

### Opening

```text
Thanks for taking the time. I’m not here to sell a finished legal product. I’m trying to understand the workflow first.

The area I’m exploring is document/admin work around legal matters: intake, document organization, missing information, summaries, follow-ups, and task preparation.
```

### Questions

Ask:

1. What repetitive intake, document, or admin task do you hate most?
2. Where does the information come from?
3. What does the finished output need to look like?
4. Who uses that output?
5. What happens if it is late, incomplete, or wrong?
6. How often does this happen?
7. How long does it take manually?
8. What tools are involved today?
9. Who currently does this work?
10. Would anything need approval before sending, updating, or filing?
11. What would you never want automated?
12. If this workflow was prepared for review automatically, what would that be worth monthly?

### Close

```text
Based on what you described, I’ll map the workflow and tell you whether I think it is a good pilot candidate.

If it is, the first pilot would stay narrow: one workflow, fake/sample or approved data first, human approval before anything sensitive, then a managed monthly plan only if it saves time.
```

## Qualification criteria

A firm is qualified if:

- they can name a painful repetitive workflow
- the workflow happens weekly or daily
- the input is messy or scattered
- the output has a recognizable structure
- manual time cost is clear
- human approval is acceptable
- they are open to a private demo/pilot
- they have budget authority or access to the person who does

A firm is not qualified yet if:

- they only want legal advice automation
- they cannot name a workflow
- the workflow happens rarely
- they need full production compliance answers before a demo conversation
- they want to upload real sensitive data into the shared demo immediately
- they are only curious and have no pain

## Objection handling

### “Is this giving legal advice?”

```text
No. The demo is not legal advice. The goal is to reduce admin/document preparation work and produce structured outputs for human review.
```

### “Can we upload real client files?”

```text
Not into the shared demo. For real files, we would discuss a private setup, access boundaries, and approval process first.
```

### “Is this your software?”

```text
The legal document workspace demo uses MikeOSS, an AGPL open-source project. ProChat installs, manages, and extends the workflow around it with ProChat OS.
```

### “Will this replace staff?”

```text
The first goal is not replacement. It is reducing repetitive admin preparation so staff can review and act faster.
```

### “Does it run automatically?”

```text
New workflows should start with human approval. Only low-risk steps should become more automatic after trust is built.
```

## What to learn from every reply

Capture exact language.

Look for repeated phrases like:

- intake is messy
- clients send incomplete information
- too much time summarizing
- missing documents
- too many follow-ups
- hard to keep track
- admin bottleneck
- paralegal overload
- document review takes too long

Those phrases should later shape landing pages, workflow names, and the managed offer.

## Success criteria for this phase

The outreach phase is successful when:

- at least one firm books a discovery call
- at least one painful workflow is clearly described
- the workflow has messy inputs and structured outputs
- the firm accepts human approval first
- the firm understands the private/managed path
- a narrow pilot can be proposed
